## Finding 1 — "Lease contention timeout" names the wrong cause

**Severity:** low impact, high diagnostic cost. This is the kind of message that
sends someone looking in the wrong place for an hour.

Observed at 21:19:06:

```
21:19:01.112  ERROR INSTR_WRITE  Transaction failed to GPIB0::7::INSTR: VI_ERROR_NLISTENERS
21:19:01.115  INFO  VISAMANAGER  Purged resource from cache: GPIB0::7::INSTR
21:19:06.122  WARN  VISAMANAGER  GPIB0::7::INSTR marked unresponsive (connect exceeded 5.0s budget)
21:19:06.123  WARN  INSTR_READ   Lease contention timeout on GPIB0::7::INSTR; treating as bus timeout.
```

There was no lease contention. The line immediately above states the real cause:
the connect budget was exceeded.

**Mechanism.** In `perform_instrument_read` two different operations are awaited
under one handler, and both raise `asyncio.TimeoutError`:

```python
try:
    await self.acquire_lease(visa_addr, client_addr, read_tmo_ms)
    res, res_lock = await self.visa_manager.async_get_resource(...)
except asyncio.TimeoutError:
    logger.warning("INSTR_READ", "... Lease contention timeout ...")
```

`acquire_lease` raising means another session holds the resource.
`async_get_resource` raising means the VISA open blew its budget. Completely
different problems, one message.

**Fix:** separate the two awaits so each reports its own cause. Wire behaviour
is unchanged — this is log-only.

## Finding 2 — connect budget is shorter than a GPIB open on an absent instrument

**Severity:** medium. Produces a 5 s stall and a spurious "unresponsive"
marking, then self-corrects.

Same window. Full sequence:

| time | event |
| :--- | :--- |
| 21:19:00.955 | `*IDN?` written to `GPIB0::7` |
| 21:19:01.112 | `VI_ERROR_NLISTENERS` — nothing on the bus at address 7 |
| 21:19:01.115 | cleanup: `clear()` failed, resource **purged from cache** |
| 21:19:06.122 | reopen **exceeded the 5.0 s connect budget** → marked unresponsive |
| 21:19:06.123 | read gives up |
| **21:19:06.255** | **the same open completes successfully, 130 ms later** |
| 21:19:06.255 | `GPIB0::7::INSTR responded; cooldown cleared` |

The open did not fail — it was *slow*. `async_get_resource` wraps
`get_resource` in `asyncio.wait_for(..., connect_budget_s)` where
`connect_budget_s = min(5.0, (timeout_ms / 1000) + 2.0)`. The `wait_for` gave up
at 5 s; the orphaned worker thread finished the open 130 ms afterwards and
cached it normally.

So NI-VISA's `viOpen` against a GPIB address with no listener takes just over
5 s on this host. The budget is too tight for that case.

**Consequences:** a 5 s stall on the client, and a cooldown recorded against an
instrument that was about to open fine. It self-corrected here because the late
success called `clear_unresponsive()`.

**Open questions before changing anything:**

- Is >5 s the normal NI-VISA GPIB open time for an absent address on this host,
  or was this a one-off? Worth timing directly.
- Should the budget scale with `read_tmo_ms` rather than cap at 5 s?
- Should a *purge* be followed by an immediate reopen attempt at all, or should
  the failure just propagate to the client?

Not changed yet — needs a measurement, not a guess.

## Confirmed working

**The read-timeout change behaves as intended.** `VI_ERROR_NLISTENERS` is a
genuine fault and was still logged at ERROR with a traceback, and still ran the
cleanup path. Only `VI_ERROR_TMO` is treated as expected. That boundary is
holding.

**Recovery worked.** After the fault, `GPIB0::8` (slot 3) answered normally on
the very next command, and `GPIB0::7` reconnected without restarting VMSG.

**Not a VMSG fault:** `GPIB0::7` returning `NLISTENERS` means nothing is
answering at that GPIB address — the supply is off or unplugged. VMSG reported
it correctly.
